## Program Identity

**Name:** Golden Heart Legacy Society

**Rationale:** Aligns with existing Golden Heart branding, feels elevated and mission-driven, and reinforces long-term impact. Creates natural synergy with the Gala — a pathway for post-Gala engagement that doesn't require an immediate financial commitment.

**Positioning Statement:**
> "The Golden Heart Legacy Society honors those who have chosen to ensure The Midnight Mission will always be here — for every person who needs us, now and in the future."

## Signature Experience: Legacy Salon Series

**Concept:** Small, hosted gatherings (6–20 people) in a home or unique venue, centered on intimate storytelling about recovery, impact, and the future of The Midnight Mission.

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Frequency | 1–2 per year |
| Format | Story-driven conversation, not a pitch |
| Speakers | David + 1 program graduate or staff |
| Venue | Donor home, board member home, or curated space |
| Invitation list | Legacy Society members + select warm prospects |
| Goal | Deepen relationships; organically grow the Society |

**Why it works:** Intimacy builds trust. Existing members become informal ambassadors. New prospects hear from people like them, not from a brochure.

## Gala Connection Strategy

The Golden Heart Legacy Society creates a natural post-Gala engagement pathway:

- Gala attendees experience the mission emotionally but may not be ready to write a major check
- Legacy giving offers a way to say *"yes, I'm in"* without immediate financial impact
- Consider: Legacy Society recognition at the Gala (table tent, mention, optional listing in program)
- Consider: Gala follow-up communication that introduces the Society to Golden Heart ticket holders

---

## Messaging Principles

- **Lead with mission, not mechanics.** Don't open with "estate plans" — open with legacy and impact.
- **Make it feel exclusive and intimate,** not transactional.
- **Honor the decision they've already made.** Once someone says yes, every touchpoint should celebrate them, not ask them for more.
- **Use their name.** Every communication should feel personal.
